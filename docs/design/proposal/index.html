Continuum· Redesign proposal · Nov 14, 2026

A calm work queue for people who close the loop after discharge

This proposal redesigns every page of the Continuum console, the landing page and sign-in, and adds the pages that are missing: sign-up by invitation, team workload, settings, and a physician view. Each page below shows today's screen next to the proposed one at the same size.

Open the clickable prototype Go straight to the console

The prototype is one HTML file with synthetic data. Nothing in the React app has changed. Screens marked "today" were captured from the running app at localhost:8000 on Nov 14, 2026.

The three decisions everything else follows from
  1. One work queue instead of a dashboard. Everything that needs a person, from an overdue follow-up to a failed import, lands in Needs you. One item is open at a time with one main button. Acting on it moves you to the next item.
  2. Every patient runs on a 30-day clock. Transitional care has fixed deadlines (contact in 2 business days, a visit in 7 or 14 days). Each patient page shows them on a timeline, and the queue sorts by them.
  3. Two colours. Ink for everything, red only for "overdue" and "out of range". Hierarchy comes from size, weight and position, not colour.

1. Who it's for

Four people use the console. The design is built for the first; the others get a view that fits how often and where they use it.

Care coordinator

Primary user · all day
Who
Nurse or social worker. 40–80 recently discharged patients.
Device
Desktop, often two monitors, headset for calls.
Day
Constant interruptions; works in short bursts between calls.
Needs
Know what to do next, hit the 2-day and 7/14-day deadlines, close items only with proof.
Gets
Needs you queue, keyboard shortcuts, hand-off.

Attending physician

Occasional · minutes a day
Who
Signed the discharge; owns results that need a clinical read.
Device
Phone or tablet between rounds, workstation on wheels.
Needs
See abnormal results fast, mark reviewed, ask a coordinator to act.
Gets
Results to review: the same queue, filtered to clinical items. Nothing else in the way.

Coordinator lead

Daily check-ins
Who
Runs the team and the handoff between shifts.
Needs
Workload per person, unowned items, deadline risk, audit for compliance.
Gets
Team page: per-person counts, unassigned work, end-of-shift note.

Integration admin

Rare
Who
IT or informatics; watches the EHR connection.
Needs
Failed imports, duplicate messages, identity questions, connection health.
Gets
Activity → Imports and Identity checks. Failures also reach the queue so nothing waits for the admin.

Patients and caregivers never see the console. They talk to the voice assistant, and what they say comes in as a report, never as proof.

4. Style

Direction: a calm work queue. It should look like a well-kept paper worklist: quiet surfaces, clear rules, strong type. Nothing decorative. The attention goes to the one thing that needs doing.

ElementRule
ColourInk #18191B for text and primary buttons · Red #B42318 only for overdue and out-of-range values · neutrals #F6F5F2 canvas, white surfaces, hairlines · blue only as the keyboard focus ring. A full dark theme uses the same names. Every text pair passes WCAG AA (lowest 5.06:1).
TypefaceAtkinson Hyperlegible Next for reading, Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono for IDs, times and lab codes. Made by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers; it separates I/l/1 and 0/O, which matters in MRNs and lab values.
ShapeRadius 4 px for controls, 6 px for panels. Hairline borders. No shadows except menus and the search dialog.
StatusA dot plus a word: red filled dot "4 days overdue", hollow ring "Due Nov 18", grey filled dot "Done". Never a coloured chip.
IconsOnly where a symbol is universal: search, notifications, menu. Navigation is words.
CopyPlain sentences about what is true: "The clinic hasn't booked Sarah." Absolute times (09:06, Nov 13). Buttons say the action: "Call the clinic", "Verify and close".
MotionOnly to explain change: the item slides out after an action (220 ms), drawers slide in (180 ms). All of it switches off with "reduce motion".

8. Header and search

The header is 56 px and the same on every console page:

  • Left: logo and unit in the sidebar (on smaller screens a "Menu" button, on phones the mark alone).
  • Centre: search, up to 560 px wide. Opens with / or ⌘K. Finds patients by name, MRN or condition, open follow-ups, and pages ("imports", "text size"). Arrow keys and Enter. On phones it's an icon that opens the same dialog.
  • Right: live status ("Live · 09:14", red "Offline" when disconnected) · notifications with a count of only the urgent tier (overdue and out-of-range results) · keyboard help · account menu (name, role, unit, display settings, switch view, sign out).

"Ask Continuum" is no longer in the header. The assistant lives where it's useful: the How decisions were made tab on each patient, with that patient's records only.

Landing page: watch it, then try it

Plan: landing-plan.md. Built around what works on strong product sites: show the real product moving, then let people use it. 279 words in total (the earlier draft had 619 and no images or video).

  • Hero video recorded from the prototype (17 s, 260 KB, silent loop, Pause button, still image under reduced motion): a coordinator opens Cardiology, verifies it, the next item opens, and a call goes out. Phones get a separate phone-layout recording. Files in prototype/landing/.
  • Play Michael's 30 days: drag a day slider or press Play (it starts once when scrolled into view). His six follow-ups close, go red and get chased; one caption and the proving record per day. Click an item or a day mark to jump.
  • Try it yourself: the live console embedded and clickable, with three hint steps (Cardiology → Verify and close → Undo).
  • Four rules as small looping animations, one line each; they stop under reduced motion.
  • Numbers with footnoted sources, a one-line "connects with / built on", and a footer with both calls to action.

Sign in

  • Hospital SSO first (how hospitals actually sign people in), email second, demo third.
  • Forgot password, keep-me-signed-in, plain error message.
  • No gradient panel or "proof" card; one sentence on the right about how access works.
  • Links to accept an invitation or request access.

Sign up (new): invitation and request access

Clinical tools aren't open sign-up: a team lead invites people. So sign-up is Accept invitation (confirm details → set password or use SSO → text size and alert preferences) and Request access for people without an invite.

Today (dashboard) → Needs you

  • Counts become a sentence above the list ("4 items · 2 overdue · overdue first, then by deadline").
  • Mine · Team · Unassigned plus filters All · Overdue · To verify · System.
  • The open item: patient line, title, status, owner ("Assigned to you · Hand off"), the why, the evidence, sources collapsed, one primary button.
  • After an action: the item slides out, toast with Undo, the next item opens.
  • Waiting on others and Done today are collapsed at the bottom.
  • The demo-day runner leaves the work screen; it belongs with other demo tools in Settings (not drawn in the prototype).

Approvals → "To verify" in the same queue

  • No separate page to check. Proof waiting for sign-off is an item like any other, under the "To verify" filter.
  • "Ordered vs. what came back" side by side, with the late date in red; the clinic note quoted; what verifying also cancels is in Sources.
  • "Verify and close" or "Not enough proof" (asks what's missing).

Patients

  • A table you can scan: patient and MRN, condition, day since discharge, next deadline, what needs you, coordinator.
  • Search and filters All · Mine · Needs you · Overdue.
  • On phones each row becomes a stacked card.

Patient journey → Patient

  • 30 days after discharge timeline: discharged, first contact, visit deadline (red if missed), seen, day 30, and today.
  • Follow-ups grouped Needs someone · Waiting on others · Done; each "Open →" jumps to it in the queue.
  • Tabs: Follow-ups · History · How decisions were made. Contact and consent as a summary list on the right.
  • "Call Michael" opens the call panel with a live transcript and the intended → sent → confirmed track.

Agent inspector → "How decisions were made"

  • Ask about this patient, then read in plain words what the assistant understood, checked, proposed and remembered.
  • Graph, checkpoint and thread IDs stay available under "Technical details" for engineers and judges.

Patient intake → Add a discharge

  • First choice: Import from the hospital record (MRN → import, follow it in Imports) or Enter it by hand.
  • By hand: Patient → Stay (sets the 7 or 14-day window) → Contact and consent → Follow-ups (suggested from the summary, each one ticked by you) → Check, with Change links.

12. Build order (React app)

  1. Tokens and fonts in styles.css (colour, rem type scale, spacing, density); the old names alias to the new ones so nothing breaks.
  2. Shell (Layout.tsx): sidebar, header with search and account menu, breakpoints, bottom tabs; replace WelcomeTour with the first-run line.
  3. Needs you (Dashboard.tsx + ApprovalsPage.tsx): queue, scopes, focus pane, resizable divider, undo.
  4. Patient (PatientJourney.tsx, Inspector.tsx): 30-day clock, grouped follow-ups, tabs, call panel.
  5. Patients, Audit log, then the console-features pages in the Activity group.
  6. Add a discharge (PatientIntake.tsx), Team, Settings.
  7. Sign in, invitation, request access, landing.

Backend additions this needs: item owner and hand-off, per-patient TCM deadlines (discharge date + business days), a notification tier per event, and invitations. The rest uses data the API already returns.
